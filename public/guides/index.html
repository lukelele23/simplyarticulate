Speaking practice guides

Use one practice take to choose one change. Then repeat the same topic.

A one-minute answer

  1. Start with your answer. Say the main point before the background.
  2. Give one example. Use something specific rather than listing every possible reason.
  3. Finish with the takeaway. Explain what you want the listener to remember.

Example topic: “Describe a small habit that improved your day.” An opening could be: “Writing tomorrow’s first task before bed helps me start faster.” Spend the rest of the take explaining your own example.

A short presentation opening

State the problem your listener recognizes, explain your recommendation and say what you will cover. Record only the opening first. Replay it and ask whether a listener could summarize the point in one sentence.

When a prompt is only a phrase

Turn “Small victories” into “Describe a small victory from this week and why it mattered.” For an abstract topic, choose one perspective and a concrete example. You can also choose another topic.

Using AI feedback

Listen before reading the score. Choose a correction you can recognize in the recording, such as a late main point or an unclear example. Repeat the same topic with that change. A different score by itself does not prove improvement; check whether the answer is easier to follow.

Pace and fillers

Use words-per-minute and filler counts as clues, not targets to maximize or minimize. A pause can help a listener understand an idea. Keep natural speech and focus first on whether your point is clear.

Debate preparation

Identify your claim, supporting reason and likely objection before speaking. Check the clock sequence against your tournament’s rules. The app’s default timings are a practice aid.
